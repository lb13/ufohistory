On the morning of **16 October 2002**, Dr. Eric W. Davis arrived at the **EG&G Special Projects building** — located at Grier and Paradise in Las Vegas, adjacent to the "Janet Terminal" at McCarran International Airport — for a meeting that had been arranged ten minutes before his arrival. Vice Admiral **Thomas R. Wilson** (US Navy, retired), former Director of the Defense Intelligence Agency, arrived shortly after in civilian dress, accompanied by a lieutenant, a commander, and a petty officer.

The two men sat in the back of Wilson's car for approximately eighty minutes. Davis took handwritten notes.

## What Wilson Described

According to Davis's notes, Wilson recounted events beginning in spring 1997, when he was still serving as Deputy DIA Director for the Joint Chiefs of Staff. While working through classified programme records in the Office of the Under Secretary of Defense for Acquisition and Technology (OUSD/AT), Wilson had located a reference to an **unacknowledged Special Access Programme** managed by a private aerospace contractor — a programme that appeared to involve the reverse engineering of recovered technology of non-human origin.

Wilson attempted to gain access through proper channels. He was refused. A **Senior Review Group (SRG)** convened and its chairman informed Wilson that he did not have "need to know," that if he continued to press the matter he would not be promoted to Director of the DIA, and that he could face early retirement with potential loss of rank. Two days later the SRG sustained the denial and instructed Wilson to drop the matter.

Wilson told Davis he had complied. He subsequently served as DIA Director and retired on **29 July 2002** — three and a half months before this meeting.

## The Document

Davis's thirteen pages of handwritten notes became known as the **Wilson-Davis memo** (or Wilson-Davis notes). They circulated privately within the UFO research community for years before being leaked publicly in June 2019 by researcher **Grant Cameron**, who had obtained them from the estate of the late astronaut **Edgar Mitchell**.

Historian Richard Dolan described the document as potentially *"the most important UFO document ever leaked to the public."*

Admiral Wilson, when subsequently asked by journalists, denied the account.

## Context

The meeting took place just three months after [Northrop Grumman completed its acquisition of TRW](/events/2002-07-01-northrop-acquires-trw) — a consolidation that concentrated the very aerospace and intelligence assets Wilson's account implied. Eric Davis had also been discussing the case with colleagues: a diary entry from **Jacques Vallée dated 3 March 2001** records Davis telling him of ten Velobind volumes at Wright-Patterson containing Roswell data and a **TRW special study** — a detail that connects directly to the alleged **Project ZODIAC** and the Sedge Masters articles published in *UFO Magazine* in 1998.
