In **spring 1997**, **Vice Admiral Thomas R. Wilson** — at the time serving as Deputy Director of the Defense Intelligence Agency for the Joint Chiefs of Staff — discovered a reference to an unacknowledged Special Access Programme while reviewing classified records held by the Office of the Under Secretary of Defense for Acquisition and Technology (OUSD/AT).

What he found appeared to be a programme managed by a private aerospace contractor involving technology of non-human origin. Wilson spent the next **45 days** attempting to gain access through proper channels.

## Sequence of Events

**9 April 1997:** Wilson is briefed at a conference room at the Pentagon, where the programme first comes to his attention.

**April–May 1997:** Wilson investigates, navigates the Special Access Programme Oversight Committee (SAPOC) bureaucracy, and attempts to use his position and clearances to gain access. He meets with **Secretary of Defense William Perry** at some point during this period.

**June 1997:** A subgroup of SAPOC members — the **Senior Review Group (SRG)** — convenes at Wilson's request. The SRG chairman informs Wilson:
- He does not have "need to know" for this programme.
- If he continues to press the matter, he will **not be promoted to Director of the DIA**.
- He faces **early retirement**.
- He could potentially **lose one or two stars** in rank.

Wilson complied. He subsequently was promoted to Director of the Defense Intelligence Agency, serving until his retirement on **29 July 2002**.

## The Disclosure — October 2002

Wilson disclosed these events to astrophysicist **Dr. Eric W. Davis** at a meeting in Las Vegas in **[October 2002](/events/2002-10-16-wilson-davis-meeting)** — five years later, three months after his retirement. Davis recorded the account in thirteen pages of handwritten notes that were leaked publicly in June 2019.

## Significance

The 1997 incident — if Wilson's account is accurate — represents a striking inversion of the normal chain of command: a three-star admiral with intelligence oversight responsibilities, acting within his official duties, was blocked from accessing a programme and threatened with career destruction by a contractor oversight group. The SRG's authority to threaten a serving flag officer suggests a classification structure operating with effective autonomy from military command.

The identity of the contractor has not been confirmed publicly. The timing — 1997 — predates the **[Northrop Grumman acquisition of TRW](/events/2002-07-01-northrop-acquires-trw)** by five years, leaving the programme potentially within TRW's corporate structure at the time of the denial.
