On **22 December 2023**, President Biden signed the **National Defense Authorization Act for Fiscal Year 2024**, which contained a substantially revised version of the **Unidentified Anomalous Phenomena Disclosure Act of 2023** — originally introduced by Senate Majority Leader **Chuck Schumer (D-NY)** and Senator **Mike Rounds (R-SD)**, modelled on the President John F. Kennedy Assassination Records Collection Act of 1992.

## The Original Bill

The Schumer-Rounds amendment, introduced in summer 2023 in the wake of the **[Grusch Congressional testimony](/events/2023-07-26-grusch-congressional-testimony)**, was ambitious:
- A **UAP Records Review Board** — an independent, nine-member, presidentially appointed body with authority to compel disclosure, modelled on the Assassination Records Review Board.
- A **presumption of immediate disclosure** for all UAP-related government records, with postponement requiring specific justification.
- **Federal eminent domain** over UAP-related materials held by private persons or entities — meaning the government could compel the surrender of recovered materials from defence contractors.
- A 25-year deadline for complete disclosure.

## What Was Stripped Out

The House Intelligence Committee insisted on removing two of the three core provisions before the bill could pass conference:
1. The **eminent domain clause** — eliminating any mechanism to compel private contractors to surrender UAP-related materials.
2. The **independent UAP Records Review Board** — leaving disclosure decisions within the existing executive branch structure rather than an independent body.

Schumer stated it was *"beyond disappointing that the House refused to work with us on all of the important elements."* Both Schumer and Rounds pledged to reintroduce the stripped provisions in future legislation.

## What Remained

The signed version established that:
- The **National Archives and Records Administration (NARA)** must collect UAP records from across the federal government.
- NARA gains a **legal mandate** to release those records to the public, subject to a review process.
- The legislation creates a formal definition of UAP records for archival purposes — for the first time bringing UAP into the statutory records framework.

## Significance

The passage and subsequent stripping of the disclosure bill illustrates the structural obstacles to UAP transparency: even with bipartisan Senate support and post-Grusch political momentum, the mechanism most threatening to corporate secrecy — eminent domain over private materials — was removed before enactment. Researchers noted that without eminent domain, any contractor holding recovered technology had no legal compulsion to surrender it.
