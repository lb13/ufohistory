In the late summer of 1948, analysts at the **Air Technical Intelligence Center (ATIC)**, Wright-Patterson AFB, working under **Project Sign**, completed a landmark — and controversial — classified intelligence document known as the **"Estimate of the Situation."**

## Contents
The Estimate was drafted primarily by project officer **Capt. Robert R. Sneider**, likely with contributions from Alfred Loedding, A.B. Deyarmond, and Lawrence Truettner. It reviewed multiple unexplained sightings by pilots, scientists, and other credible witnesses, and formally concluded that the extraterrestrial hypothesis (ETH) was the most plausible explanation for the UFO phenomenon.

Among the supporting arguments advanced:
- The flight characteristics of reported objects (speed, manoeuvrability, absence of sonic boom) exceeded the capabilities of any known Soviet or American aircraft.
- Multiple high-quality witnesses — military pilots, radar operators, scientists at White Sands — had reported objects behaving in ways inconsistent with known atmospheric phenomena.
- The Estimate noted that UFO reports seemed to correlate with the approach of planets (Mercury, Venus, Mars) to Earth, suggesting these might be used as "launching bases."

## Approval and Rejection
The Estimate was approved internally by Cols. William Clingerman and Howard McCoy, then submitted up the chain to General **Charles Cabell**, newly appointed chief of Air Force intelligence. Cabell's office passed it to **General Hoyt S. Vandenberg**, Chief of Staff of the U.S. Air Force.

Vandenberg rejected the document, reportedly on the grounds that the conclusions — that UFOs were interplanetary craft — were extraordinary claims unsupported by physical evidence. The internal response was described by later accounts as an "uproar."

## Aftermath: Destruction of Copies
All copies of the Estimate were ordered destroyed. According to researcher Michael Swords, who examined the original draft of Edward Ruppelt's manuscript, Ruppelt's *published* account omitted significant documentation proving the document's conclusions — suggesting Air Force censorship of even Ruppelt's contemporaneous account.

Personnel who refused to abandon the extraterrestrial hypothesis were reassigned. Project Sign was dissolved and replaced by [Project Grudge](/events/1949-02-11-project-grudge) in February 1949, with a mandate oriented toward debunking rather than investigation.

## Significance
The Estimate represents the first and only time an official U.S. government intelligence body produced a formal document concluding that UFOs were extraterrestrial. Its suppression and destruction, and the subsequent reassignment of Sign's most open-minded analysts, has been cited as the foundational act in establishing the institutional stigma surrounding the subject.

> *"The estimate was rejected — it was batted back down the chain of command with a note that it was not at all the proper conclusion."*
> — Edward J. Ruppelt, *The Report on Unidentified Flying Objects* (1956)
