On **26 July 2023**, **David Charles Grusch** testified under oath before the House Committee on Oversight and Accountability's National Security Subcommittee in a hearing entitled "Unidentified Anomalous Phenomena: Implications on National Security, Public Safety, and Government Transparency." He was joined by former Navy pilots **Ryan Graves** and **David Fravor**.

Grusch was a 14-year intelligence officer — US Air Force (Major) and, from 2021 to 2023, a GS-15 civilian analyst at the **National Geospatial-Intelligence Agency (NGA)**. He had previously served as a representative to the UAP Task Force and the All-domain Anomaly Resolution Office (AARO).

## Key Claims

**Multi-decade crash retrieval programme:** Grusch testified that he *"was informed in the course of my official duties of a multi-decade UAP crash retrieval and reverse-engineering program"* — run with secrecy operating above congressional oversight.

**Non-human biologics:** He stated that the United States had recovered *"non-human biologics"* from the pilots or occupants of recovered craft.

**Over 40 witnesses:** Grusch said his conclusions were based on interviews with more than 40 current and former military and intelligence community officials conducted over four years.

**Illegal concealment:** He alleged that members of Congress and appropriate oversight bodies had been deliberately denied information about these programmes in violation of law.

## The O'Sullivan and Clapper Allegation

Among Grusch's most specific claims was that **Stephanie O'Sullivan** — Principal Deputy Director of National Intelligence under DNI James Clapper (2011–2017) and now chairman of [The Aerospace Corporation](/events/1960-06-03-aerospace-corporation-founded) — had managed the crash retrieval issue alongside Clapper at ODNI. O'Sullivan's prior career at TRW and her subsequent intelligence and FFRDC roles place this allegation within a coherent institutional network.

## Whistleblower Process

Grusch made his disclosures through the proper legal channel: a **PPD-19 Urgent Concern** filing with the Intelligence Community Inspector General. The ICIG found his complaint *"credible and urgent."* He subsequently filed a complaint alleging illegal retaliation, which was also accepted by the ICIG.

## Significance

The hearing represented the most explicit on-record government-connected testimony asserting the existence of crash retrieval programmes. Unlike earlier accounts from researchers or retired military figures, Grusch spoke as a named, credentialed, active-clearance intelligence officer, under oath, with a formal whistleblower complaint on record. The Congressional setting and the "credible and urgent" finding by the ICIG gave the testimony a formal weight absent from previous disclosures.
