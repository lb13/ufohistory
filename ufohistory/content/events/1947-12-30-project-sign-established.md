On 30 December 1947, Major General L.C. Craigie, Director of Research and Development, issued the order formally establishing **Project Sign** — also initially known as **Project Saucer** — at the Air Technical Intelligence Center (ATIC), Wright Field (later Wright-Patterson AFB), Dayton, Ohio.

The project grew directly from the [Twining Letter of September 1947](/events/1947-twining-letter), in which General Nathan Twining had declared the "flying disc" phenomenon "something real and not visionary or fictitious" and urged a permanent investigation be launched. Project Sign was given a **2A priority** (the highest possible being 1A), reflecting the seriousness with which high-ranking officers initially regarded the phenomenon.

## Formal Start
The project formally began operations on **23 January 1948** under the direction of Col. Howard M. McCoy, head of ATIC's T-2 Intelligence Division. The project officer was **Capt. Robert R. Sneider**.

## Mission
Project Sign was tasked with collecting, collating, evaluating, and distributing to interested government agencies and contractors all information concerning reported UFO sightings. The dominant working hypotheses at the start were either:
1. The objects were advanced Soviet secret weapons; or
2. The objects were of interplanetary origin.

The Soviet hypothesis was quickly weakened — flight characteristics of reported objects exceeded any known Soviet capability. This shifted serious internal debate toward the extraterrestrial hypothesis.

## Significant Cases Investigated
Project Sign evaluated **243 reported UFO sightings** during its active period, including:
- The [Mantell Incident (January 1948)](/events/1948-mantell-incident): Kentucky Air National Guard pilot Capt. Thomas Mantell died chasing a UFO; initially described as a "metallic object of tremendous size."
- Multiple sightings by military personnel over Muroc AFB, White Sands Proving Grounds, and other sensitive installations in 1947.

## The Estimate of the Situation
In **August–September 1948**, Project Sign's team drafted a classified intelligence summary — the *[Estimate of the Situation](/events/1948-09-estimate-of-the-situation)* — formally concluding that the flying objects were of interplanetary (extraterrestrial) origin. The Estimate was rejected by Gen. Hoyt Vandenberg, Chief of Staff, USAF, on the grounds of insufficient physical evidence, and all copies were ordered destroyed.

## Transition to Project Grudge
After the rejection of the Estimate, Sign personnel who refused to abandon the extraterrestrial hypothesis were reassigned. The project was renamed **[Project Grudge](/events/1949-02-11-project-grudge)** in February 1949, with a markedly different — largely debunking — mandate.
