On **15 July 2022**, the Department of Defense formally established the **All-domain Anomaly Resolution Office (AARO)** within the Office of the Secretary of Defense. The announcement came via a Deputy Secretary of Defense memorandum. AARO replaced and absorbed the **Unidentified Aerial Phenomena Task Force (UAPTF)**, which was simultaneously disestablished by the Secretary of the Navy, with all data, analyses, and materials transferred to the new office.

## Mandate

AARO was given authority to detect, identify, and attribute UAP sightings across all physical domains: air, sea, space, and land — a deliberately broader remit than the Navy-focused UAPTF. Its responsibilities include:

- Coordinating cross-agency UAP detection and reporting
- Standardising data collection across military services
- Providing regular classified and unclassified reports to Congress
- Managing a secure reporting mechanism for military and government personnel

## Structure

AARO operates within the Office of the Under Secretary of Defense for Intelligence and Security (OUSD I&S), reflecting the dual classification of UAP as both a safety and counterintelligence matter. It coordinates with the Director of National Intelligence, the military services, and the intelligence community.

## Context

AARO's creation followed the June 2021 **ODNI Preliminary Assessment on UAP** (which acknowledged 144 unexplained incidents without resolving any) and congressional pressure for a permanent, adequately resourced investigation body. The 2022 **National Defense Authorization Act** mandated the creation of a successor to the UAPTF.

The office opened hundreds of investigations in its first two years. A public-facing website was launched on **31 August 2023**, one month after the [Grusch Congressional testimony](/events/2023-07-26-grusch-congressional-testimony).
