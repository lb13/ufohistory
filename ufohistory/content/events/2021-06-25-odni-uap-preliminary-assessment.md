On **25 June 2021**, the **Office of the Director of National Intelligence (ODNI)** released a nine-page unclassified *Preliminary Assessment: Unidentified Aerial Phenomena*, produced by the **UAP Task Force (UAPTF)**. The report had been mandated by the Senate Intelligence Committee in the FY2021 Intelligence Authorization Act, with a 180-day deadline.

## Scope

The assessment covered **144 UAP reports** collected primarily from US Navy and military personnel between **2004 and 2021**. Almost all reports originated from US government sources; the report explicitly noted it could not assess the full scope of the phenomenon as civilian reports were outside its remit.

## Key Findings

**143 of 144 incidents unresolved.** The UAPTF could explain only one of the 144 incidents with high confidence — identified as "a large, deflating balloon." The remaining 143 lacked sufficient data for definitive attribution.

**18 incidents with anomalous flight characteristics.** Eighteen reports described objects that "appeared to remain stationary in winds aloft, move against the wind, maneuver abruptly, or move at considerable speed, without discernible means of propulsion." The report noted some released radio frequency energy detected by US military sensors.

**Five possible explanatory categories:**
1. Airborne clutter (balloons, birds, UAVs, debris)
2. Natural atmospheric phenomena
3. US government or industry developmental programmes
4. Foreign adversary systems
5. A residual "other" category

The report explicitly declined to rule out any category, and noted that UAP *"probably lack a single explanation."*

**Data collection problems acknowledged.** The assessment identified stigma around reporting as a structural problem suppressing incident data — a direct echo of the conclusion reached by the CIA and Air Force's earlier active suppression efforts beginning in the 1950s.

## Congressional Context

The report was mandated by the Intelligence Authorization Act for FY2021, following pressure from Senate Intelligence Committee chairman **Marco Rubio** after the New York Times' December 2017 reporting on AATIP and the November 2004 USS Nimitz encounter. The classified annex accompanying the unclassified report has not been released.

## Significance

The ODNI assessment was the first official, publicly released US government document since the **1969 closure of Project Blue Book** to formally acknowledge that a substantial body of unexplained military UAP incidents existed and could not be attributed to known phenomena. Its release marked a shift from the official posture of the preceding fifty years — from dismissal to ambiguous acknowledgement.

The report directly preceded the **[establishment of AARO](/events/2022-07-15-aaro-established)** in July 2022 and set the legislative context for the **[Grusch Congressional testimony](/events/2023-07-26-grusch-congressional-testimony)** in July 2023.
