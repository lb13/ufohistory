On **8 March 2024**, the **All-domain Anomaly Resolution Office (AARO)** released *Report on the Historical Record of U.S. Government Involvement with Unidentified Anomalous Phenomena (UAP) — Volume I*, covering the period from 1945 to the present. AARO acting director **Tim Phillips** presented the findings at a Pentagon briefing on **6 March 2024**.

## Key Findings

The report concluded:

- **"No verifiable evidence"** that any UAP sighting has represented extraterrestrial activity.
- **No evidence** that the US government or private industry has ever had access to extraterrestrial technology.
- Alleged hidden UAP programmes either **do not exist** or were **misidentified** legitimate national security programmes unrelated to extraterrestrial technology.
- Claims from whistleblowers were attributed largely to the **misidentification of classified programmes** for other purposes, rumour, and good-faith but mistaken belief.

## Controversy

The report generated immediate and sustained criticism from UAP researchers, former officials, and some members of Congress:

**Grusch ignored:** The report did not engage substantively with the testimony of **David Grusch**, who had testified under oath before Congress in [July 2023](/events/2023-07-26-grusch-congressional-testimony) — as a named, credentialed, active-clearance intelligence officer, with a formal whistleblower complaint found "credible and urgent" by the Intelligence Community Inspector General. Critics noted that the report assessed UAP history without interviewing Grusch or his 40+ sources.

**Curated documentary record:** Analysts argued the report carefully selected which historical documents and programmes it addressed, omitting material relevant to programmes named in whistleblower accounts, and that its methodology was circular — finding no evidence of programmes that are by definition unacknowledged.

**Conflict of interest:** AARO is housed within the Office of the Under Secretary of Defense for Intelligence and Security — the same organisational structure alleged to oversee the programmes it was assessing.

**Volume II:** A second volume was announced but had not been published as of early 2025.

## Structural Context

The report's publication came eight months after the Grusch testimony and amid continued legislative efforts to strengthen UAP disclosure mechanisms. The stripping of the eminent domain clause from the [UAP Disclosure Act](/events/2023-12-22-uap-disclosure-act) three months earlier meant that even if programmes existed within private contractors, there was no legal mechanism to compel the surrender of materials — and AARO had no authority to demand access to unacknowledged corporate programmes.
