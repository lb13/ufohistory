Title: Stephanie O'Sullivan
Description: Former CIA Directorate of Science and Technology director, Principal Deputy Director of National Intelligence (2011–2017), and current chair of The Aerospace Corporation board; named by David Grusch in 2025–2026 media appearances as having managed crash retrieval issues alongside DNI James Clapper.
---

**Stephanie L. O'Sullivan** (born 3 October 1959) is a former senior US intelligence official whose career moved from TRW to the Office of Naval Intelligence, the CIA, and ODNI, before transitioning to a range of private sector and non-profit board roles — most notably as chair of the board of **The Aerospace Corporation**, the FFRDC spun out of TRW in 1960.

## Career

**TRW (~1982 to late 1980s)**
O'Sullivan graduated with a B.S. in Civil Engineering from Missouri S&T in 1982 and moved to Annapolis, Maryland, where she responded to a classified TRW help-wanted advertisement seeking an "ocean engineer." She worked at TRW as a member of the technical staff and then as a programme manager. The exact end date of her TRW employment is not in the public record; the transition to ONI occurred before she joined CIA in 1995.

**Office of Naval Intelligence**
O'Sullivan served at the Office of Naval Intelligence between her TRW and CIA careers. No title or date range has been specified in public sources.

**CIA (1995–2011)**
O'Sullivan entered the CIA in 1995. Over more than 16 years she accumulated:
- **Directorate of Science and Technology (DS&T):** More than 14 years across her CIA career, including approximately four years as **Director, DS&T** (roughly 2005–2009). The DS&T manages the CIA's technical collection and exploitation capabilities — human implant technology, SIGINT, overhead systems, and classified scientific programmes.
- **Associate Deputy Director of Central Intelligence:** Appointed by Director Leon Panetta in December 2009, succeeding Scott White. Served in this role until her ODNI nomination in January 2011.

**Principal Deputy Director of National Intelligence (January 2011 – January 2017)**
Nominated by President Obama on 5 January 2011 at James Clapper's recommendation. Confirmed unanimously by the Senate Intelligence Committee on 15 February 2011. At the time she was the most senior woman in the US intelligence community and the second-highest-ranking intelligence official in the United States government. She served for six years alongside DNI Clapper.

## Post-Government Roles (from 2017)

- **The Aerospace Corporation:** Joined the board of trustees on 13 June 2017. Elected vice chair December 2019. Elected **chair of the board effective 11 June 2021** — the operational head of the FFRDC that was itself created by splitting off TRW's Space Technology Laboratories in 1960.
- **Battelle Memorial Institute:** Elected to the board 28 March 2018.
- **HRL Laboratories** (formerly Hughes Research Laboratories, now a joint venture of Boeing and Northrop Grumman): Board director; listed as chairman of the board in some sources.
- **Huntington Ingalls Industries:** Elected to the board effective 15 January 2021.
- **In-Q-Tel:** Trustee.
- **Booz Allen Hamilton:** Advisory board.
- **Georgetown University Center for Security and Emerging Technology:** Adjunct faculty.
- **National Academy of Engineering:** Elected member 2019.

## UAP Allegations

O'Sullivan is not named by David Grusch in his **July 2023 Congressional testimony** — that public testimony did not name specific individuals in connection with programme management.

Her name emerges in two later contexts:

1. **David Grusch's Megyn Kelly interview (late 2025):** Grusch alleged that Clapper "placed people in critical roles to manage this issue" and named O'Sullivan in this connection.

2. **Liberation Times report (24 January 2026):** Citing three unnamed sources, the report alleged that O'Sullivan co-managed an alleged programme codenamed **"Golden Domes"** — described as a CIA/USAF joint operation — while serving as DS&T Director, and that she "misled the Senate Select Committee on Intelligence, including then-Senator Marco Rubio, by denying involvement."

These are journalistic allegations based on named whistleblower testimony and unnamed sources. They are not confirmed by any congressional record or official document.

Her career arc — TRW → Naval Intelligence → CIA DS&T → ODNI → Aerospace Corporation — traces the exact institutional lineage that UAP researchers identify as central to the alleged legacy programme network.
