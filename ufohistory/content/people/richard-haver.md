**Richard L. Haver** is a career intelligence professional whose arc — Naval Intelligence → Office of the Secretary of Defense (twice, under Cheney and Rumsfeld) → TRW → Northrop Grumman — places him at the nexus of the institutions most frequently named in connection with alleged UAP legacy programme management.

## Career

**Education and Early Naval Service**
- B.A. in History, Johns Hopkins University (1967)
- US Navy active duty 1967–1973 as a Naval Aviator, flying reconnaissance missions in the Western Pacific during the Vietnam War

**Naval Intelligence (civilian, 1973 onward)**
- 1973: Entered civilian intelligence as an analyst, Anti-Submarine Warfare Systems branch, Naval Intelligence Support Center
- 1976: Department head, Navy Field Operational Intelligence Office (NFOIO)
- 1977: Technical Director, Naval Ocean Surveillance Information Center
- Subsequently: Technical Director, Naval Operational Intelligence Center; Special Assistant to the Director of Naval Intelligence
- Became the **first civilian to serve as Deputy Director of Naval Intelligence** — a milestone confirmed by his later PASSUR Aerospace press release biography

**Identification of the John Walker Spy Ring**
Haver is credited as the intelligence analyst who identified the Soviet penetration of US Navy communications codes that the **John Anthony Walker Jr. spy ring** was later confirmed to have facilitated. Walker's espionage, ongoing from 1968 to 1985, gave the Soviets access to Navy cipher systems; Haver's analytical work on the ASW anomalies that Walker's intelligence enabled helped expose the operation.

**Special Assistant for Intelligence, OSD — First appointment (1989)**
In 1989, Secretary of Defense **Dick Cheney** selected Haver to fill a newly created position: **Special Assistant for Intelligence in the Office of the Secretary of Defense**. This was a handpicked role created specifically for him, giving him direct access to and advisory influence over Cheney on intelligence matters at the highest levels of the defence establishment.

**Executive Director, Intelligence Community Affairs (1992–1995)**
Haver served as Executive Director for Intelligence Community Affairs (EXDIR/ICA) on the Director of Central Intelligence's staff — a senior coordinating role within the broader IC structure.

**Further IC Roles (late 1990s)**
Held multiple roles including: Damage Assessor in major espionage cases; Chief of Staff, National Intelligence Council; National Intelligence Officer for Information Operations; Deputy to the Assistant Director of Central Intelligence for Analysis and Production.

**TRW — Vice President and Director, Intelligence Programs (1999)**
In 1999 Haver joined TRW as **VP and Director, Intelligence Programs**, leading business development and marketing for intelligence accounts within TRW's Systems & Information Technology Group, and serving as liaison to the C3, telecom, and Space & Electronics groups.

**Northrop Grumman — Vice President, Intelligence Programs (2002–December 2010)**
When Northrop Grumman completed its [acquisition of TRW in July 2002](/events/2002-07-01-northrop-acquires-trw), Haver continued in the same intelligence programmes leadership role within Northrop. He held the VP Intelligence Programs position until his **retirement in December 2010** — spanning eleven years of combined TRW/Northrop service from 1999.

**Special Assistant for Intelligence, OSD — Second appointment (2001)**
During the 2000–2001 presidential transition, Cheney again selected Haver — this time to head the **Bush-Cheney intelligence transition team**. Secretary of Defense Rumsfeld then appointed him **Special Assistant to the Secretary of Defense for Intelligence** (2001–approximately 2003). In this role, Haver was a primary architect of the legislation creating the **Under Secretary of Defense for Intelligence (USDI)** position (bill passed November 12, 2002; signed December 2, 2002). Stephen Cambone was appointed USDI, not Haver.

**Post-Northrop**
- **PASSUR Aerospace:** Joined the board of directors October 8, 2010, overlapping with his Northrop retirement. PASSUR is an aviation predictive analytics company.
