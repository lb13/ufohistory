Title: Dr. Edward Bushnell Doll
Description: Electrical engineer, Manhattan Project veteran (Project Alberta fusing team leader), Stanford Physics chairman (1953), named by witness Arthur Stansel Jr. as the coordinator who summoned engineers to the 1953 Kingman, Arizona crash retrieval; alleged later career at TRW Systems Group.
---

**Edward Bushnell Doll** (born 24 September 1912; died 17 February 1998, buried Santa Barbara Cemetery, California) was an electrical engineer whose career moved from the Manhattan Project through Stanford University and Stanford Research Institute, and — according to UAP researcher accounts — into senior roles at TRW Systems Group. He is named in Kingman crash retrieval accounts as the authority who summoned engineer Arthur Stansel Jr. to the May 1953 operation.

> **Note on spelling:** His surname is "Doll," not "Dole." The "Dole" spelling appears in early UFO researcher write-ups (including derivatives of Raymond Fowler's research) and has propagated widely, but verifiable institutional records — Atomic Heritage Foundation/Nuclear Museum, Find a Grave, newspaper obituary — all use "Doll."

## Career

**Manhattan Project — Project Alberta (1945)**
Doll served as the **Fusing Team Leader** for Project Alberta, the Manhattan Project component responsible for assembling, transporting, and readying the first operational atomic bombs at Tinian Island (Mariana Islands). He worked alongside Ashworth, Kirkpatrick, Ramsey, and Parsons on the operational preparation of the weapons used in August 1945.

**Stanford University — Chairman of Physics Department (1953)**
In 1953 — the same year as Operation Upshot-Knothole and the alleged Kingman incident — Doll became **Chairman of the Physics Department at Stanford University**. This is confirmed by both the Atomic Heritage Foundation profile and Kingman-incident research based on Fowler's follow-up. He subsequently moved to the Stanford Research Institute (SRI), an applied research organisation with substantial government and defence contracts.

**Operation Upshot-Knothole (March–June 1953)**
Doll served in an advisory capacity at the 1953 nuclear test shots at the Nevada Test Site. Accounts based on Raymond Fowler's Kingman research describe him as test director for the AEC's Operation Upshot-Knothole atomic tests at Frenchman's Flat — though the precise title varies across partially sanitised AEC/DOD records. The operation ran from **17 March to 4 June 1953**, spanning the period immediately before and during the alleged Kingman retrieval on 21 May 1953.

**TRW Systems Group (alleged, 1955–1977)**
UAP researcher accounts, including the UAP Gerb analysis, allege that Doll joined TRW's predecessor organisation in 1955 and rose through the executive ranks (VP → SVP → EVP, TRW Systems Group) before retiring in 1977. This career trajectory has not been independently verified in publicly available corporate records. Southern California newspaper references from 1962–63 place him in the TRW/Redondo Beach area during this period, which is consistent but not confirmatory.

## Kingman, Arizona — May 1953

The sequence is notable. The Upshot-Knothole test series was still ongoing when Arthur Stansel Jr. noted in his diary on **20 May 1953**: *"funny call from Dr. D about 1000. I'm going on a special job tomorrow."*

Stansel — an engineer in the Air Material Command's Installations Division, working within the Office of Special Studies under Dr. Eric Henry Wang at Wright-Patterson — was transported by blacked-out bus to a desert site near Kingman, Arizona, where he witnessed a recovered oval craft and two non-human bodies. He identified **"Dr. D" as Dr. Edward Doll** — the official who coordinated his participation in the operation.

Stansel disclosed the account in 1973 to researcher Raymond Fowler under the pseudonym "Fritz Warner," signing a sworn affidavit on **7 June 1973**. He dropped the pseudonym before his death in 2006.
