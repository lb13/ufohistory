**Edward Bushnell Dole** (1912–1998) was an electrical engineer whose career moved from the Manhattan Project through Stanford Research Institute and on to senior executive roles at TRW, placing him at the intersection of classified weapons research, nuclear effects testing, and — according to witness testimony — alleged UAP crash retrieval operations.

## Career

**Manhattan Project — Project Alberta**
Dole participated in Project Alberta, the component of the Manhattan Project responsible for delivering the atomic bombs used against Japan in August 1945. Project Alberta handled ordnance assembly, ballistic testing, and the operational preparation of the weapons at Tinian Island.

**Stanford Research Institute (SRI)**
Following the war, Dole held positions at Stanford and at the Stanford Research Institute — an applied research organisation with deep government contracts in defence, electronics, and intelligence.

**Operation Upshot-Knothole (March–June 1953)**
In early 1953 Dole directed the **military effects group** during Operation Upshot-Knothole, a series of eleven nuclear detonations at the Nevada Test Site. The military effects group measured the impact of nuclear detonations on personnel, equipment, and structures under realistic combat conditions, and coordinated the roughly 650–720 DOD personnel assigned to effects testing on individual shots. The operation ran from **17 March to 4 June 1953**.

**TRW Systems Group (1955–1977)**
Dole joined TRW's predecessor organisation in 1955 and progressed through the senior executive ranks:
- **Vice President**, TRW Systems Group
- **Senior Vice President**
- **Executive Vice President**

He retired from TRW in 1977, having spent more than two decades at the firm through its critical growth period building ICBMs, satellites, and classified defence systems.

## Kingman, Arizona — May 1953

The sequence of events in May 1953 is of particular interest. The nuclear test series was still ongoing (it would not conclude until 4 June) when Arthur Stansel Jr. recorded in his diary on **20 May 1953**: *"funny call from Dr. D about 1000. I'm going on a special job tomorrow."*

Stansel — an engineer in the Air Material Command's Installations Division, working within the Office of Special Studies under Dr. Eric Henry Wang — was subsequently transported by blacked-out bus to the Kingman, Arizona desert, where he witnessed a recovered oval craft and two bodies. He named **"Dr. D" as Dr. Edward Dole** — the official who coordinated his participation in the operation.

Stansel disclosed the account in 1973 to researcher Raymond Fowler under the pseudonym "Fritz Warner," signing a sworn affidavit on **7 June 1973**.

Dole's background — Manhattan Project weapons assembly, nuclear effects testing, and his imminent move into TRW senior leadership — places him precisely in the network of engineers and administrators that UAP researchers have identified as the institutional core of alleged crash retrieval activity in the early 1950s.
